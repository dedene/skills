# 50 — AI search, answer engines, and the "website won't matter" thesis

Wes's view moved fast between December 2024 and September 2026. Separate what he **claims** (predictions, often with single-source stats and a commercial motive; he now sells YouTube training) from what a site should **do now**.

## The 2026 thesis in detail `[W:VXGDHZIGf40 2026]`

- **Discovery is conversational.** People describe their whole situation to ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude (often by voice) and ask for a shortlist, instead of typing 2–3 keywords (43% have used an AI assistant to research a purchase; 1 in 4 say AI is their main source of recommendations; illustrative).
- **Where AI gets answers.** YouTube is claimed to be the most-cited source in Google's AI answers (~30% of AI answers include a video; illustrative). His theory: owner-controlled sites are easy to game, big third-party platforms carry more weight.
- **AI gets you into the consideration set; people still choose.** Most use AI to compare and narrow options, and most double-check elsewhere (53% / 85%; illustrative). When they double-check, they look for an expert talking it through: stories, cases, "what happened with the person in their exact situation". The #1 destination after chatbots is YouTube (Similarweb figure; illustrative).
- **Case:** a Tennessee gutter installer gets nearly all business via AI search because of his YouTube videos (comparisons, tests, installation tutorials); the models learned his specialty and service area.
- **AI citation ≠ popularity.** Whether a video is cited has "virtually no relationship to its views, likes or channel size" (unnamed study; illustrative); machines read content the day it's posted.
- **Copy is commoditized.** "When everyone's site is great, having a great site stops being the reason for someone to pick you." The differentiator is what AI can't fake: you, and watching you think through a problem.
- **Niche is now required.** When someone describes a specific situation, the specialist is easy to pick out: "when you're for everybody, there's no question that you're going to be the obvious answer to." Wes asked AI for an accountant who specializes in course sellers, not "a good accountant".
- **The site shrinks.** With content building confidence upstream, most businesses can go from a ~30-page "mega site" to one landing page (or a few for several offers): smaller, less salesy, still well written, still good-looking, still showing how you help. It still must exist ("you do still need" one).
- **Window:** ~12 months before competitors catch up and learn to "game" AI recommendations (prediction).

## What to do now (rules for the skills)

Wes's advice is mostly about YouTube. Translated to the website, with [G] where it goes beyond him:

**AI-01 — Publish the "logistics answers" on the site too.** Wes's first video type, drawn from sales calls and the inbox, one question per piece `[W:VXGDHZIGf40 2026]`:
- **Price:** "How much does [service] cost in [area]?" with the closest thing to a real number and what moves it up or down (PRICE-07).
- **Comparisons:** you vs the alternative (agency vs in-house; "an agency telling you when not to hire an agency builds trust").
- **Process:** "How to get ready for your first session", what happens step by step.
- **Timelines:** "Why does X take so long?", "How long until I see results?"
- **How to choose / questions to ask / red flags.**
On the site: one page or a solid FAQ entry per question, plain answer in the first paragraph, then detail, then the CTA. Mirror YouTube topics if you make video. Write for humans; "the machines will just end up getting everything they need anyway".

**AI-02 — Send niche signals ("claim jumpers").** `[W:VXGDHZIGf40 2026]` Stake out the specialty and ideal client: case studies of your exact right client (who, what you did, what changed), repeated across pages; topic selection that only your niche would care about; casual client mentions ("when I was working with a business coach last year who…"). On the site: service pages per niche/situation, case studies tagged by client type and town, a positioning statement in plain words (BRIEF-07).

**AI-03 — Make the business facts unambiguous and consistent [G].** AI assistants assemble answers from many sources. State plainly on the site what you do, for whom, where (service area by town), prices or ranges, languages, opening hours. Keep name, address, phone, categories and services identical across the site, Google Business Profile and directories; add LocalBusiness/Service structured data; keep text as real HTML text (not baked into images); keep reviews flowing on third-party platforms. *Evidence level:* practitioner consensus, not measured; don't promise rankings.

**AI-04 — Assume some visitors arrive pre-sold.** Make the booking path obvious from any page, keep the hero's CTA visible on mobile, and don't force warm visitors through a long argument; a short page variant or a "Ready to talk? Book here" block high on the page helps them `[W:VXGDHZIGf40 2026]`. Keep the full argument for cold visitors below (C18).

**AI-05 — Put the human on the page.** Video of you explaining, real team photos, stories in your words, your inner thought in case stories. This is the part AI can't fake, and what double-checkers look for `[W:VXGDHZIGf40 2026, R7jjzeKgCYU 2023, bWVyN9VoBB4 2026]`. Embed your own logistics and case videos on the matching pages `[W:1kgolxbvCd8 ≤2024]`.

**AI-06 — Ask how they found you.** Add "ChatGPT/AI assistant" and "YouTube" options to the "How did you find us?" field; referral data from AI tools is otherwise mostly invisible in analytics [G] (MEAS-02 in the `conversion-audit` skill's `audit-checklist.md`).

**AI-07 — Use AI features where they remove a sales conversation, not as decoration.** `[W:_2gU7G5U99I 2025, lxndWdjOyVM 2026]`
- AI chat trained on your services, prices, FAQs and past chat logs, with your tone configured (out of the box it "sounded exactly like AI").
- AI estimator that accepts plain-language input and returns a range (LEAD-13).
- Scheduling assistant that books by your rules and sends reminders (BOOK-03).
- Micro-app as the transitional offer (LEAD-14).
- Skip for now: video-avatar twins (immature in 2025), custom GPTs hosted on someone else's platform.
[G/BE]: an AI chat that gives quotes or advice is still your statement to consumers; constrain it to published prices and hand off to a human for anything binding. Disclose that it's an AI assistant.

**AI-08 — Don't:** keyword-stuff headlines (it never persuaded humans `[W:64ie_-xuMgQ 2021]`); mass-publish generic AI blog posts (buried in the flood `[W:db4tr3BAWJo 2024]`); rely on blog SEO as the traffic engine (2020 advice now reversed `[W:XMrQ-NEuZ7o 2020, QJnhnhXuuYM 2025]`); treat the site as the place to "win" on design alone.

## How pages change

| Page element | Before (2018–2024 emphasis) | Now (2025–2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage length | Full blueprint for cold traffic | Full blueprint *or* shorter for warm traffic; booking always one click away |
| FAQ | ~6 objection-answers | Plus the logistics answers (price, process, timeline, comparisons, how to choose), each answerable in its first sentence |
| Pricing | Ranges/factors to filter leads | Also the "closest real number" for AI answers |
| Case studies | Proof section | Also niche signals: exact client type, town, situation |
| Lead magnet | PDF → quiz | Quiz, estimator, micro-app; delivered on your domain |
| Human presence | About page, team photo | Video explanations, founder voice, visible specifics; the non-fakeable layer |
| Copy quality | The main lever | Necessary but table stakes; differentiation comes from proof, niche, method, person |
| Discovery | SEO + ads + social | AI assistants + YouTube + GBP + referrals; site = closer |

## Open questions (flag, don't assert)

- Whether AI assistants really favor YouTube over websites for local services varies by assistant and country; Wes's stats are US-centric and unsourced. In Belgium, test: ask the main assistants the questions your customers ask (in NL and FR) and record who gets named [G].
- Whether a single landing page is enough depends on having a content engine that actually runs. Most SMBs don't publish weekly video; for them the persuasive site remains the main salesperson (C18).
