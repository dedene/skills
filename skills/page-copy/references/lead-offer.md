# 30 — Lead offer: transitional offers, lead magnets, quiz, micro-apps, nurture, scarcity, offer design

**Why this exists.** Most first-time visitors are comparison-shopping, not buying (Wes's estimates: "only 10–20% ready" in 2018 → "96% never going to buy on the first visit" in 2023; illustrative) `[W:_hBEZT8-uOw 2018, jPclFEiC2o4 2023, K5is_yACb7M 2024]`. Returning visitors convert far better (1.5% vs 12.6%, "8×", from a 2006 study; illustrative) `[W:cqh4JDnX6KU 2022]`. So the site has a second job: build a "runway of future clients" with a lower-commitment offer and email follow-up `[W:3ahiFlIHvDc 2022, jPclFEiC2o4 2023]`. "Instead of asking how can I get people to come to my website, the real million-dollar question is how do I get them to come back" `[W:cqh4JDnX6KU 2022]`.

## The two-path rule

**LEAD-01 — Primary CTA for the ready; transitional offer for everyone else.** Keep the primary action; add the lead magnet as a clearly secondary path `[W:_hBEZT8-uOw 2018, XMrQ-NEuZ7o 2020, jPclFEiC2o4 2023, bWVyN9VoBB4 2026]`. Never make the lead magnet or quiz the main CTA `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`.

**LEAD-02 — Foot in the door.** A small commitment (an email for a freebie) makes the bigger one (booking) more likely; use the thank-you page to present the bigger step `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018]`.

## What makes a good lead magnet

**LEAD-03 — Solve a slice of the problem they'd hire you for.** Pick one small part of the customer's big problem and solve it for free; it shows competence and reveals the next problem, which you solve for money ("every problem has a solution; every solution reveals more problems", Hormozi) `[W:oy6gte9oWVo 2024]`. Plumber: how to unclog a sink; when it gets worse or bigger, they call him `[W:1RGMHttqQN4 2019]`. Realtor: "how to increase your home's value" before listing `[W:oy6gte9oWVo 2024]`. **Not a hiring guide:** "Your guide to finding a better financial advisor" isn't a lead magnet; a 3-day budget starter is `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`.

**LEAD-04 — Promise a specific result, not general knowledge.** Formula: **result + constraint (cost, time, situation)**: "Free at-home exercise ebook" → "How to set up your own at-home gym for under $100" `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`. Add insider angle and curiosity ("5 insider secrets…" beats "5 things to know before hiring a contractor", which reads like a blog post) `[W:1RGMHttqQN4 2019]`. If a quick search gives the same thing free, it isn't worth an email `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`.

**LEAD-05 — Promise doing, not learning.** "Learning what it takes is not the same as doing something." A "4-day challenge" described as "a sneak peek into our course" breaks the promise; "four daily assignments that, done together, put you well on your way to rebuilding your relationship" keeps it, and then you actually send them `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`.

**LEAD-06 — Title and description must match.** Mismatch makes people ask "what is this actually?", which kills opt-ins `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`.

**LEAD-07 — Not a disguised brochure.** The first touch should wow them with a quick win they tie to you; promote later `[W:1RGMHttqQN4 2019]`. Exception: mini-webinars and longer trainings may end with a soft pitch after real value; guides and checklists may end with a soft next step ("now that you know all this, if you want one-on-one help, schedule a consultation") `[W:1RGMHttqQN4 2019]`.

**LEAD-08 — Format fits the content and the person.** Physical/movement topics → video, not PDF; a busy parent → audio for the commute, not a 2-hour webinar `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022, 1RGMHttqQN4 2019]`. Quality beats length: his best performer was "just a checklist", because "people love to be told exactly what to do in what order" `[W:1RGMHttqQN4 2019]`. `[MS]`: consumable in under ~10–30 minutes, one format, works on mobile.

**LEAD-09 — "Personalized" must be real.** If you claim personalization, collect inputs (a ~5-question quiz) and generate a genuinely tailored plan `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`.

**LEAD-10 — "Join our newsletter" is not a lead magnet.** It promises "an inbox full of spam" `[W:1RGMHttqQN4 2019, db4tr3BAWJo 2024]`.

**LEAD-11 — Retitle around the reader's result.** A case study titled "learn how we helped create their vision" → "The behind-the-scenes way one company used its branding to double sales in 30 days, and how you can replicate their results". Often only the title needs to change. Mine your own bullet points; the best hook is often buried `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`. Plain language even for specialist audiences: "5 indispensable skills to become the CMO of tomorrow (and make sure you're never out of a job)" `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`.

## Match to buyer stage `[MS, adapted to SMB]`

| Stage | Visitor thinks | Transitional offer examples |
|---|---|---|
| Problem-aware | "Is this a real problem? Can I fix it myself?" | Checklist, 5-day challenge, DIY quick-win video ("unclog your sink") |
| Solution-comparing | "Who/what should I choose?" | Comparison guide (with "doing nothing" and DIY), "questions to ask before hiring", red-flags checklist, package-finder quiz |
| Decision-ready but hesitant | "What will it cost me? Will it work for me?" | Price estimator/calculator, sample plan, mini case-study pack, free assessment or audit, a paid pilot |

Wes's service-business magnets span all three, but the later ones cluster in the decision stage (estimators, quizzes that recommend a package, pricing calculators) `[W:_XmzVYmVfJM 2024, 2lYdI86YV30 2023, _2gU7G5U99I 2025]`.

## Formats

**LEAD-12 — Quiz / assessment.** Interactive, curiosity-driven, longer first visit `[W:vTDe5SblNUk 2023]`. Needs a "juicy payoff": a quote, personalized plan, score, or tailored recommendation (Wes: a playlist of his most relevant videos; "2,000+ leads a year", illustrative). **Capture the email just before revealing the result** `[W:vTDe5SblNUk 2023]`. Neil Patel's line: "Answer 9 quick questions and I'll give you a step-by-step 7-week action plan…" `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`. Doubles as a package finder for confusing tiers `[W:2lYdI86YV30 2023]`. Style quizzes work for design businesses (a client: 666 → 3,852 leads in under a year; illustrative) `[W:RiNJ-rl4MSU 2022]`. `[MS]`: 5–10 multiple-choice questions (~7), progress indicator, 3–5 result types, follow-up emails tailored by result.

**LEAD-13 — Estimator / calculator.** Visitors enter needs and get a ballpark; they feel in control, expectations are set, leads arrive pre-qualified, and you skip calls with people who can't afford you `[W:_XmzVYmVfJM 2024, _2gU7G5U99I 2025]`. AI versions accept plain-language input ("a 1960s house, about 120 m², want natural stone") `[W:_2gU7G5U99I 2025]`. Gating: C13 (ballpark open, personal estimate gated).

**LEAD-14 — Micro-app (2026).** A simple interactive tool where people enter their info and get something personal back, which they return to; it does a job they already do "slower and worse" `[W:lxndWdjOyVM 2026]`.
- Scope test: "If you can't describe your tool as 'It takes X and gives me Y,' start over." One useful action in a simple interface, not a full app.
- Idea prompt: "What annoying thing does my ideal client already do manually and badly? What part feels dumb, slow or repetitive?"
- Build: brain-dump business + ideal client → pick from ~5 ideas by "which makes my clients' lives easiest" → light style direction → **logic validation** with your real pricing/method data so the output is expert-grounded, not invented → generate a build prompt → AI app builder → iterate → publish on your branded domain, not a temporary URL.
- Utility over looks. Examples: freelancer pricing calculator with live price points; side-hustle matcher; traffic-potential calculator (agency); AI-readiness quiz with an action plan (claim: 37,000 leads in 90 days; illustrative).
- Positioning edge: most established competitors still use stale PDFs, so a working tool makes you look more credible and generous ("that gap won't last forever").
- Distribution: "the demo is the content"; a video demonstrating the tool converts 10–20% vs 1–2% for a passing mention (illustrative). Aimed at expertise businesses; for trades, a simple estimator is the equivalent.

**LEAD-15 — Custom-GPT lead magnet (2025, dated).** A GPT trained on your knowledge and writing sample, gated by email (trainer workout plans, contract red-flag checker, style quiz) `[W:_2gU7G5U99I 2025]`. Depends on visitors having ChatGPT accounts and on OpenAI's platform; prefer a micro-app on your own domain.

**LEAD-16 — Mini-webinar / on-demand training.** ~10 minutes teaching one specific problem + a 3–5-minute soft pitch positioning the paid service as "the next logical step"; gate it with email so an automated sequence can follow, "where the bulk of your conversions are going to come in" (ungated option: a dedicated page with only the player, linked in the nav) `[W:qvbLxQsic4w 2022]`. Must deliver the promised value; no hard sell.

**LEAD-17 — Timed challenge.** 5/10/30 days, one actionable email or video a day, stacking toward a stated result; builds habit and relationship `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`. Wes's favorite quick-win type in 2025: a "5-minute declutter challenge" `[W:QJnhnhXuuYM 2025]`.

**LEAD-18 — Checklist, template, swipe file, curated resource list.** Low effort, "a small but important result on their own"; make a big deal of it on the site (Lilach Bullock's email swipe file: "56%+ of visitors"; illustrative) `[W:jPclFEiC2o4 2023]`. `[MS]`: checklist 1–2 pages, actionable verbs, workflow order; templates usable within 5 minutes.

**LEAD-19 — Video series / pre-sell article** as warm-up for prospects not yet aware they need a pro `[W:WktIWzpeMis 2020]`.

## Placement and promotion

**LEAD-20 — On the site:** its own section lower on the homepage with a simple form `[W:_hBEZT8-uOw 2018]`; prominent and repeated on every page `[W:t4gF4aVRcDY 2024]`; as a text link under the hero CTA (homepage, C1); contextual on content pages (the matching content upgrade) `[MS]`; exit-intent popup on desktop ("before you go…"), never an instant popup `[W:_hBEZT8-uOw 2018, 1RGMHttqQN4 2019, 7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`.

**LEAD-21 — Off the site:** email signature, social bios, the matching YouTube video ("match the lead magnet to the video topic") `[W:1RGMHttqQN4 2019, db4tr3BAWJo 2024]`. As site traffic shrinks, passive magnets don't work; drive people to them proactively `[W:db4tr3BAWJo 2024]`. Paid promotion of the magnet is "much better ad spend" than advertising core services directly `[W:1RGMHttqQN4 2019]` (2019 Facebook-era advice; [G/BE]: needs cookie consent and a clean consent trail, `be-eu.md`).

## Opt-in page

`[MS]` structure + Wes's rules:
1. Headline = the specific result + constraint (LEAD-04), matching the button that brought them here
2. Visual of the deliverable (cover, screenshot, sample result)
3. 3–5 bullets: what they'll achieve, get or be able to do `[W:ZhGY67SPk4w 2022]`
4. Proof: download count, a testimonial about the freebie
5. Form: email only (+ first name if you'll personalize); button "Send me the checklist" / "Show my results"
6. Microcopy: what they'll receive and how often you'll email; unsubscribe anytime
7. FAQ if needed (format, really free?)

For warm traffic from video: headline + email field + button, nothing else `[W:lxndWdjOyVM 2026]`.

**Delivery:** thank-you page confirms and gives instant access + an email copy (verifies the address, starts the relationship) `[MS]`; the automated handoff email delivers the freebie `[W:lxndWdjOyVM 2026]`. The freebie must deliver on its promise immediately, like a grocery-store sample `[W:cqh4JDnX6KU 2022]`.

## Nurture sequence (3–5 emails, then ongoing)

Principles:
- **Help first, then ask.** GaryVee's "jab, jab, jab, right hook": three helpful emails, then one promotional `[W:jPclFEiC2o4 2023]`. Get more promotional by email 3–4 and ask directly: "you can't do this dance forever" `[W:cqh4JDnX6KU 2022]`. Sales pitch "about four emails later" `[W:3ahiFlIHvDc 2022]`.
- **Tease in the email, link to the site.** Each email teases a piece of content and why it matters, and links to the full article/video on your site; each click is the 2nd, 3rd, 4th visit, and each content page has its own CTA `[W:cqh4JDnX6KU 2022]`.
- **Story → point → bridge.** Drop into a moment mid-action (no preamble) → brief context → the point → bridge to one action. Financial planner: dentist waiting room, rescheduled three times → avoidance comes from fear → "that's why people put off finances; it's not laziness, it's fear" → "just reply to this email. No judgment." `[W:bWVyN9VoBB4 2026]`.
- **Subject lines from pain phrases:** "Feel like you're constantly putting out fires?" beats "5 tips to scale your business" `[W:rByridDOAKM 2025]`.
- **Give far more than you ask** while you're growing (≥ 10:1 give:ask for growing audiences) `[W:oy6gte9oWVo 2024]`.
- Weekly helpful email "for as long as it takes them to decide"; high-ticket buyers can sit on a list for years `[W:XMrQ-NEuZ7o 2020, RiNJ-rl4MSU 2022]`.

Template sequence (adapt per business; timing [G]):

| # | When | Purpose | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Immediately | Deliver + first win | The freebie, one instruction to get the quick win today, what to expect from these emails. Soft P.S. with the booking link |
| 2 | Day 2 | Jab: empathy + insight | Story → point → bridge on the #1 pain; link to a site page (process or logistics answer) |
| 3 | Day 4 | Jab: proof | A client story in the reader's situation (Situation, Desire, Conflict, Change, Result); link to the case study |
| 4 | Day 6 | Jab: objection | Answer the #1 objection (price, time, "will it work for me"); link to pricing/FAQ; include the "I know my clients were worried about…" frame `[W:qvbLxQsic4w 2022]` |
| 5 | Day 8 | Right hook | Direct invitation: what the call/visit is, what they get, duration, cost, booster line, honest availability (real capacity only) |
| → | Weekly | Ongoing | Helpful content, 3:1 help:promo, re-invite every ~4th email |

Retargeting is the second return mechanism (pixel, warm-audience ads, "the most profitable ads you'll ever run") but iOS 14 and privacy changes weakened it `[W:cqh4JDnX6KU 2022, XMrQ-NEuZ7o 2020]`; in the EU it also requires consent [G].

## Scarcity done right

**SCAR-01 — Only true scarcity.** "We want now what we think we may not be able to get in the future" (Weinstein). It raises perceived value and speeds decisions, and it fits services *better* than products because capacity is genuinely limited `[W:q_YbF26ji7w 2023]`. Fake scarcity backfires: "people will pick up on that" `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018, IQtpeWeVqt0 2026]`. `[MS]`: the buyer should be able to verify it; if you must fake it, the offer is the problem. [G/EU]: fake time limits are a blacklisted unfair practice (`be-eu.md`).

**SCAR-02 — Four honest types for services** `[W:q_YbF26ji7w 2023]`:

| Type | Wording / example |
|---|---|
| Supply (capacity) | "I only take on five projects at a time." / "My team only works with 5–10 clients at any given time. We do have an opening coming up; we'd love to talk to see if we're a good fit." (claim: +40% close rate; illustrative) · "I only take on four new clients per month" · "My calendar opens once a quarter" `[W:IQtpeWeVqt0 2026]` · waitlists |
| Demand | "Most popular" / "Best seller" label on a tier or in proposals: a decision shortcut |
| Limited edition | Seasonal spin or bundle on existing services with an all-in price: plumber "Winterize your pipes", vet "Golden Paw" senior check-up, tutoring "Finals Focus" package |
| Time | Real deadlines, event countdowns; usually a discount, so use sparingly and time it to slow periods (movers 20% off midweek; painters "buy one room, get one free" in winter). Frequent promos train people to wait |

**SCAR-03 — Application friction, lightly.** "Apply to work with us" makes winning a spot feel like a prize, but only "a little tiny bit of friction"; a huge questionnaire kills it `[W:q_YbF26ji7w 2023]`.

**SCAR-04 — Honest urgency formats `[MS]`:** cohort/start dates, a bonus that really expires, a scheduled and communicated price increase, seasonal closing ("renovation bookings for spring close on…"). Never: countdown timers that reset, "only 3 spots left" that stays at 3, invented "X people viewing", weekly "last chance" emails.

## Offer design (when the offer itself is weak)

Wes: "The right offer sells itself with a lot less marketing effort" `[W:QJnhnhXuuYM 2025]`. `[MS]`: better copy on a weak offer compounds slowly; fix the offer first.

**OFFER-01 — Value equation** (Hormozi; via `[MS]` and Wes's book summary `[W:oy6gte9oWVo 2024]`): Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort & sacrifice). Score each 1–10; fix the lowest. SMB levers: name the dream outcome in the customer's words; raise likelihood with proof, a named method and a guarantee; cut time-to-first-result (a fast first visit, a same-week plan); cut effort (done-for-you, fewer decisions, you handle permits and cleanup).

**OFFER-02 — Package and name it.** Research what people already pay to solve and the gaps competitors leave; package as a named, structured offer with sessions, deliverables and a price (a closet installer's "Total Home Organization Blueprint", $750–1,000; a landscape designer's "Master Plan Package" delivered by Zoom for local crews to execute) `[W:QJnhnhXuuYM 2025]`. A signature method name makes the approach "pointable" `[W:VxkGExMwZCc 2026]`.

**OFFER-03 — Pre-test objections.** List the likely objections (remote skepticism, cost vs DIY/handyman, time) and answer each on the page and in the FAQ before launch `[W:QJnhnhXuuYM 2025]`.

**OFFER-04 — A consult-offer landing page must make clear** what they get, remove risk and uncertainty, give a reason to act now, and build confidence in you and your process `[W:QJnhnhXuuYM 2025]`.

**OFFER-05 — Starting from zero:** first five clients free, then a discount ladder raising the price every five clients; do it only for a new business or new service `[W:oy6gte9oWVo 2024]`. `[MS]` warns that discounting to acquire anchors you as cheap; the ladder is acceptable because it ends and earns proof.

**OFFER-06 — Avoid `[MS]`:** bonus inflation ("€5,000 of value" on a €300 service), over-promising guarantees, course-bro aesthetics on a serious trade, and "limited time" without a date.
