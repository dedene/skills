# 23 — Proof, trust, guarantees, pricing transparency

Trust is "the biggest factor" in choosing a provider `[W:rSWSXAFv1Gg 2021]`, and "distrust is the new default" `[W:att9LfaZBos 2023]`. Proof works when it is real, specific and arrives at the moment of doubt. Fake-feeling elements are contagious: "if your website's first impression feels fake, people are automatically going to think any claims you make must be fake too" `[W:t4gF4aVRcDY 2024]`.

## Testimonials: selection

**PROOF-01 — Choose stories, not compliments.** A testimonial should be a mini-story: pain before, what they couldn't do, what they can do now, or a common objection overcome. "Very professional, highly recommend" sells nothing `[W:K5is_yACb7M 2024, 7kG8otQJfws 2021]`. Structure: before → finding you → after results `[W:b0KQaZkDBKU 2018]`. Best example: "I was worried this would be a 7-month process… within 3 days I already felt better" `[W:OtChoVhEI8Y 2025]`.

**PROOF-02 — Results over process.** Clients care about the result, not how it got there: cut the review about young, energetic workers; keep "my garden looks immaculate" `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. Outcome praise beats "punctual, easy booking" praise `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021]`.

**PROOF-03 — Each testimonial proves one specific claim or kills one objection.** Six short testimonials = six benefits or objections handled, not six versions of "great service" `[W:Yd8HdycDiHI 2018]`. The DJ who claims a packed floor shows the review about the packed floor `[W:bYZkDOwQxfg 2022]`. Pick testimonials that repeat your core message (truth effect) `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018]`.

**PROOF-04 — Mirror the target customer.** People trust people like them: a woman in her 50s won't relate to a 24-year-old man's testimonial `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018]`. Match each prospect situation with a testimonial from someone in that exact situation (Amy Porterfield pattern) `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`. With multiple personas, label or filter testimonials by segment `[W:Yd8HdycDiHI 2018]`.

**PROOF-05 — Human-sounding language.** Jargon-heavy testimonials ("truly streamlined communication with attendees") read as repurposed or fake. Interview clients to capture off-the-cuff language `[W:Yd8HdycDiHI 2018]`.

## Testimonials: format and placement

**PROOF-06 — Format:** result headline pulled from the quote ("Save two hours a week"; "Only join if you want to go up to the next tax bracket") + a few sentences + name, context (town, situation) + **photo** + five-star graphic `[W:Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025, 2ncy12qlabw 2023, kZdXTq5uOEI 2021]`. Text-only testimonials get skipped; a photo is "necessary for authenticity" `[W:Yd8HdycDiHI 2018, 2ncy12qlabw 2023]`. No multi-paragraph blobs `[W:2ncy12qlabw 2023]`. A landscape background behind a quote makes it not read as a testimonial; put a face next to it `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021]`. [G/BE]: get written permission for names and photos; show the source and date for platform reviews (`be-eu.md`).

**PROOF-07 — Place proof next to the claim it proves.** When you state a benefit, the matching testimonial sits right there, "at the moment of doubt"; never scattered randomly `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`. Also near CTAs and forms `[MS]`.

**PROOF-08 — Proof map for a homepage:**
- Hero: stars + count, client faces, one stat or mini quote (`HERO-11`) `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024, Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]`
- Benefits: one proof per benefit
- Testimonials section: 1 featured (video if possible) + 3 short; top 3 by big results or objection-busting `[W:K5is_yACb7M 2024, Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]`
- Near the final CTA and forms: a short quote or rating `[MS]`
- Footer: credibility logos, "on every page and where people naturally look to contact you" `[W:3ahiFlIHvDc 2022]`
- A dedicated testimonials page linked in the nav for the rest `[W:K5is_yACb7M 2024, 3ahiFlIHvDc 2022]`
- Paid LPs: 3–5 testimonials on the page itself, no link away `[W:kZdXTq5uOEI 2021]`

**PROOF-09 — Video testimonials and case studies.** Gold standard ("very hard to fake") `[W:Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]`. Make them a story with B-roll, not a talking head saying "I enjoyed the service" `[W:Yd8HdycDiHI 2018]`. **TL;DW headline:** put the most compelling line as a headline above or beside the player, because many won't press play `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`. Video case study arc (more control than a testimonial): client's problem → what they tried and why it failed → found you → what you did → result with numbers; talk to camera + slides + optional B-roll (a dog trainer's client playing with a calm dog) `[W:qvbLxQsic4w 2022]`. Premium services: open with a story-form video case, follow with a few text testimonials `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021]`.

## Numbers, volume and individuals

**PROOF-10 — Specific, unrounded numbers.** Numbers increase credibility (a news-credibility study; illustrative); exact numbers feel "too specific to be a guess" (precision bias): "Join over 700 million registered users" `[W:RlMBBbXzQ84 2023]`. Stats section ideas: clients helped, five-star review count, years, jobs completed, money saved, time saved, awards; let numbers do the bragging so text doesn't have to `[W:bYZkDOwQxfg 2022, trfDoGoXNsE 2025]`. A selectivity stat signals quality ("rigorous screening that eliminates 96% of applicants") `[W:OtChoVhEI8Y 2025]`. Use "show, not tell": a screenshot of a real result beats a plain stat `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024]`.

**PROOF-11 — Crowd and individual together.** Bandwagon ("Join hundreds of happy customers", many five-star reviews, the busy restaurant) positions you as the safe choice; the identifiable-victim effect (one or two detailed real stories with photo or video) creates the emotional connection. Use both `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018, IH6wsGmgi8s 2023, jsOoglMSpdY 2024]`. In spoken/written content: specific outcome stories plus volume ("after working with hundreds of couples") plus tenure as an effort proxy ("20 years figuring out how to explain this") `[W:IQtpeWeVqt0 2026]`.

**PROOF-12 — Logos and media.** Client logos for B2B; cover art of podcasts you produced; "as featured in" only for **earned** media `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024, g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. Wes twice promotes buying a paid press release to claim NBC/Fox logos ("a PR stunt plain and simple", affiliate link) `[W:rSWSXAFv1Gg 2021, mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`. **Do not follow**: widely seen as low-credibility and arguably misleading to consumers [G] (C15). Third-party review widgets (WeddingWire-type) add credibility because they come from a third party `[W:7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`; link out to all Google reviews with a button rather than an obtrusive review pop-up `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021]`.

**PROOF-13 — Live "X just booked" notifications: test only, never fake.** Wes recommends them (UseProof, +10–15%; illustrative) `[W:_hBEZT8-uOw 2018, dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018, mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`, but they read as gimmicky in 2026 and are illegal when invented [G] (C14).

## Humans, place and polish

**PROOF-14 — Show the humans behind the business.** Faceless sites feel "scammy, fly-by-night" (supermarket vs farmers market) `[W:t4gF4aVRcDY 2024]`. Put one real photo of you/the team in the solution section, a name, the city; missing team, names and city create a trust barrier, especially for expensive purchases (Wes had to check LinkedIn to see if a firm was real) `[W:Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025, g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. Don't hide that you're small to look like an agency `[W:2ncy12qlabw 2023]`.

**PROOF-15 — Founder's note.** A short personal note on the homepage or sales page (a good spot: right after the pricing table): "if you made it this far, here's what to expect and what we're about". Write it by answering "why should I sign up?" as you would in one minute `[W:2ncy12qlabw 2023]`.

**PROOF-16 — Setting.** Make it obvious where you are (map, exterior, service area); "it's an indication of spam if you cannot tell where that business is" `[W:att9LfaZBos 2023]`.

**PROOF-17 — Design polish is proof.** Tight grid, sharp images, consistent style; a homemade look makes people assume the service is equally sloppy (halo effect) `[W:RiNJ-rl4MSU 2022, g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`.

**PROOF-18 — Quotation marks add credibility.** "The most powerful key on your keyboard" (Crestodina): quote real people, and use video so words, face and tone line up `[W:att9LfaZBos 2023]`.

**PROOF-19 — Transparency effect.** Admitting limits raises trust: "this doesn't work for everyone"; an agency telling you when *not* to hire an agency `[W:IQtpeWeVqt0 2026, VXGDHZIGf40 2026]`.

## When you have no testimonials yet

**PROOF-20 — Fallback ladder** (best first):
1. **Earn them fast:** new business or new service → first ~5 clients free in exchange for honest feedback, reviews and referrals; then a discount ladder (next 5 at 80% off, then 60%…), raising the price every five clients, which also creates real urgency `[W:oy6gte9oWVo 2024]` (Hormozi via Wes). [G] Keep reviews unconditional: asking for a review is fine, paying or discounting *for a positive* review is not (`be-eu.md`).
2. **Collect what you already have:** ask past clients for one tangible result and quote it; start gathering Google reviews now `[W:RlMBBbXzQ84 2023, rSWSXAFv1Gg 2021]`.
3. **Borrow credibility honestly:** niche statistics about the problem, credentials and certifications (placed low), memberships, insurance `[W:RlMBBbXzQ84 2023, bYZkDOwQxfg 2022]`.
4. **Show the work and the person:** process transparency (3-step process, what's included), founder video, portfolio, comparison chart, a guarantee `[W:K5is_yACb7M 2024, 1kgolxbvCd8 ≤2024]` `[MS]`.
5. **Never:** invented testimonials, stock "customers", fake counters. Wes's own demo used placeholder numbers and says so; real sites must use real proof `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`.

## Comparison tables

**PROOF-21 — Compare to supply missing context; you set the goalposts.** Laypeople can't judge credentials; a comparison makes value legible (Spanish tutor vs a month of Duolingo/Rosetta Stone) `[W:vTDe5SblNUk 2023]`. Include "doing nothing" or the old way ("sometimes your biggest competition… is them doing nothing") `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`. Standard vs us ("since your property isn't exactly like your neighbor's, we dig a little deeper") `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021]`.

**PROOF-22 — Keep it balanced.** Don't fill competitor columns with red X's; show where alternatives are fine and highlight your real differentiators; it should feel like educating. You must still have more checks `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024, Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]`. Rows = the buyer's biggest decision questions (price, time, results, risk). [G/BE]: comparative claims about named competitors must be objective and verifiable; compare with categories ("a typical agency", "DIY") rather than naming firms.

## Guarantees and risk reversal

Wes covers risk reversal only as the booster line under the CTA, "free" first steps, and specific promises ("1-hour response guarantee") `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024, n5Sk2sz8E2Y 2022, RlMBBbXzQ84 2023]`. The guarantee design below is `[MS]`, adapted to local services.

**GUAR-01 — Match the guarantee to the buyer's biggest perceived risk.** "What if it doesn't work?" → satisfaction/redo or conditional refund. "What if you're late or sloppy?" → service-level promise. "What if I pay and get nothing?" → pilot or milestone payment. "Is this real?" → specificity and proof, not a louder guarantee `[MS]`.

**GUAR-02 — SMB-friendly guarantee types** `[MS, adapted]`:

| Type | Example for a service SMB | Good for | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Service-level | "Quote within 48 hours or your first visit is free"; "on site within 2 hours for emergencies" | Trades, agencies | You can't measure or honor it |
| Redo / make-it-right | "Not happy with a room? We repaint it free within 30 days." | Painters, cleaners, landscapers | Subjective outcomes you can't cap |
| Conditional refund | "Do the 4 sessions and the exercises; if nothing changed, we refund session 4." | Coaching, therapy-adjacent, training | Conditions pile up (max 2) |
| Paid pilot / first-phase exit | "Month 1 is a pilot; if we miss milestone X, you don't pay for month 2." | Retainers, consultants | Vague milestones |
| Price lock | "Your quote is fixed; no surprise extras without written approval." | Renovation, contractors | You can't control scope |
| No guarantee, strong proof | "We don't do refunds; the intake call makes sure it's a fit." | Premium, application-based | First-time buyers in the category |

**GUAR-03 — Write it specifically and put it where the decision happens.** Plain terms, confident tone, near the CTA and in the FAQ, not buried in terms. Avoid "100% satisfaction guaranteed" without terms and stacks of conditions. Strong proof + a modest guarantee beats a big guarantee + weak proof. Stress-test: what if 10% invoke it? `[MS]`. [G/BE]: a commercial guarantee never replaces the legal warranty for consumers; don't word it as if it did (`be-eu.md`).

## Pricing transparency

**PRICE-01 — Address price on the site.** Hidden prices plant doubt (a menu without prices: "is this too expensive, what are they hiding?"), make prospects assume you'll charge the maximum, and send them to the first competitor that answers `[W:_XmzVYmVfJM 2024, 2ncy12qlabw 2023]`. "Being mysterious and coy about your prices doesn't create intrigue, it just creates bounce rate" `[W:ClD037qJSTQ 2025]`. The stance goes back to 2018: publish prices "even if you use custom quotes" (video description only; no transcript available) `[W:0c1Pu4Lc08M 2018]`. Anchor case: River Pools & Spas published the answer to "how much does a fiberglass pool cost?" and became the most-visited pool site (sales claims illustrative) `[W:_XmzVYmVfJM 2024]`. 2026: competitors already know your prices, so "the only person you're hiding your prices from is the one trying to decide whether to hire you" `[W:VXGDHZIGf40 2026]`.

**PRICE-02 — Rebut the three owner objections** `[W:_XmzVYmVfJM 2024]`: "I need to explain value first" (usually backfires; explain value next to the price); "competitors will undercut me" (they already know); "hidden price = negotiating room" (prospects assume the max and leave).

**PRICE-03 — Price display by pricing type** `[W:_XmzVYmVfJM 2024, ClD037qJSTQ 2025, 2ncy12qlabw 2023]`:

| Pricing type | Show |
|---|---|
| Fixed packages | A pricing page with full details per package |
| Variable | "Starting at" prices, ranges per tier ("Tier 1 typically €X–Y"), cost factors |
| Custom / project | Factors that push cost up or down (scope, materials, expertise), plus **mini price case studies**: what we did + what it cost, at several price points |
| Complex, many variables | A calculator or estimator that returns a ballpark from a few inputs (AI versions accept plain-language answers `[W:_2gU7G5U99I 2025]`) |
| Truly bespoke, no numbers allowed | At minimum: packages, process and deliverables spelled out; "not knowing what's included is a conversion nightmare" |

**PRICE-04 — Price is a filter.** After posting ballpark ranges, Wes got "no fewer inquiries, just better-qualified ones", and calls started at "how can we meet your needs" instead of justifying price `[W:_XmzVYmVfJM 2024]`. Visitors qualify themselves in or out before the consult `[W:7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`.

**PRICE-05 — Gate only the personal detail (C13).** River Pools required an email to see calculator results and got "100–200 leads a day" in peak season (illustrative; a later video gives different numbers) `[W:_XmzVYmVfJM 2024, _2gU7G5U99I 2025]`. Resolution: ranges and factors stay public; gate the saved, personalized estimate.

**PRICE-06 — Tier presentation.** Three tiers: a basic one, a premium "all the bells and whistles" tier you won't sell many of (a real anchor, not a decoy), and a middle "best of both worlds" (compromise effect) `[W:IH6wsGmgi8s 2023, dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018]`. List the most expensive first on pricing pages (anchoring: the $10k room before the $4k one) `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018]`. Label one tier "Most popular" as a decision shortcut `[W:q_YbF26ji7w 2023]`. Make tier differences crystal clear with a comparison table, "or they'll choose nothing" `[W:vTDe5SblNUk 2023]`. Too many look-alike packages → a package-finder quiz `[W:2lYdI86YV30 2023]`.

**PRICE-07 — Pricing in the AI era.** Make the "how much does X cost?" answer explicit on the site (and in video): the closest thing to a real number, plus what moves it up or down. That's "the shape of the answer the AI is going to want to give" `[W:VXGDHZIGf40 2026]`. See `ai-search.md`.

[G/BE]: consumer prices include VAT; see `be-eu.md`.
