# 10 — The brief: what `.agents/business-context.md` must capture

The brief is upstream of everything. Wes's own build order is always: messaging first, then copy for every page, then colors/images, then lead magnet, then build `[W:jPclFEiC2o4 2023, 2lYdI86YV30 2023, hDgjahAAi0g 2023]`. "Focus on content that sells, then design for it" `[W:jPclFEiC2o4 2023]`. A shared context file that every skill reads first, asking only for gaps, comes from `[MS]`.

## BRIEF rules

**BRIEF-01 — Read before asking; auto-draft from what exists.** If a site, repo, reviews page or GBP listing exists, draft the context from it and ask the owner to correct it. *Why:* faster and less tiring for the owner; owners describe their business in insider language, while the existing site and reviews often hold better lines. Wes: "most of my rewrites were lifted from points already on their site but buried" `[W:64ie_-xuMgQ 2021, nfFlB0pmsWc 2020]` `[MS]`.

**BRIEF-02 — Exactly one ideal customer per page.** Name one person in concrete detail (situation, constraints, location, life stage), e.g. "30-something single professionals who own a dog, work crazy hours, live in Chicago" `[W:trfDoGoXNsE 2025]`. Evaluate the site through that persona's eyes: Wes's "Betty", a homeowner searching at night after the kids are asleep, overwhelmed, unsure who to trust `[W:K5is_yACb7M 2024]`. *Why:* vague inputs produce vague copy; testimonials, images and words must mirror this person `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018]`. *Except:* several segments are allowed when the offer is the same (tutoring for primary and secondary). Record them, and let the hero show variety (multi-image layout and HERO-13 in the `page-copy` skill's `hero.md`). *B2B / complex sale:* the "one person" is a role in an organisation. Record buyer vs user role, organisation type and size, and the buying group (champion, decision maker, blockers such as IT, finance or the DPO); life stage and self-image are B2C fields `[G]`.

**BRIEF-03 — Capture an anti-persona.** Who is *not* a fit (price shoppers, DIY-ers who want free advice, out-of-area)? *Why:* pricing, scarcity and qualifying fields exist to filter them out; Wes repeated "strategy matters" until pretty-website-only inquiries dropped `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018]` `[MS]`.

**BRIEF-04 — Define the site's one job and the primary action.** "The website is best used just to get people on the phone or into the funnel"; closing happens offline `[W:7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`. Record the primary action (book a free consult, request a quote, call), **what happens after the click** (scheduler? callback within X hours? on-site visit?), and its cost to the prospect (free? duration?). *Why:* CTA wording, booster line and process Step 1 all derive from this `[W:Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]`.

**BRIEF-05 — Define the transitional offer.** One lower-commitment offer for the not-ready majority, tied to the paid service (see the `lead-offer` skill's `lead-offer.md`). Record: format, title, promise, delivery, and the first nurture email.

**BRIEF-06 — Record the offer mix to decide the homepage type.** List services with rough revenue share. ≥ ~80% from one offer → the homepage is that offer's landing page; one slightly dominant → route to separate pages with visual weight on the flagship; equal → "pick a lane" router with one headline covering both `[W:_hBEZT8-uOw 2018, 7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`. The homepage services section shows the 3–5 services that make ~80% of revenue `[W:Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]`. No revenue split yet (new brand or sub-brand): decide by the strategic priority offer and record why `[G]`.

**BRIEF-07 — Capture the positioning statement and signature method.** Positioning = what you do specifically + for whom + the result ("I help expertise-based business owners get their next 100 clients through YouTube"). Signature method = your specific order, emphasis or approach, **named** ("a great framework with no name, it's just advice") `[W:VxkGExMwZCc 2026]`. Also a one-liner under 20 words stating the problem solved and the outcome, used everywhere `[W:QJnhnhXuuYM 2025]`. *Why:* generic positioning makes you a commodity, so prospects shop around and question price. *Caution:* don't put the method's name in the hero; an unexplained proprietary term "creates more questions than answers" `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. Use it in the solution and process sections.

**BRIEF-08 — Capture the "why us" and the #1 objection.** Differentiators competitors lack or don't mention `[W:b0KQaZkDBKU 2018]`; the most common objection, which gets its own "wild card" homepage section `[W:hDgjahAAi0g 2023]`; the alternatives the buyer compares you with, **including doing nothing / DIY** `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`. Map competitors as direct, secondary (different solution, same problem) and indirect `[MS]`.

**BRIEF-09 — Pricing stance.** Choose one: exact price list / "from" prices / tier ranges / cost factors + example jobs / estimator. Record any "most popular" tier and a genuine high anchor tier `[W:_XmzVYmVfJM 2024, ClD037qJSTQ 2025, dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018]`. Default when the owner resists: ranges + factors (the `page-copy` skill's `proof-trust.md`, PRICE rules).

**BRIEF-10 — Build a proof inventory before writing.** Copy can only claim what proof supports ("Prove it" sweep `[MS]`). See the inventory template below. If proof is thin, plan to gather it (`PROOF-20`).

**BRIEF-11 — Mine customer language; prefer unprompted words over surveys.** People give expected, "professional" answers in surveys ("growth mindset") but write their real feelings in DMs, reviews and comments ("I'm tired of second-guessing every decision") `[W:rByridDOAKM 2025]`. Sources: own reviews, competitors' reviews, forums, Reddit, Facebook groups, sales-call notes, emails, chat logs `[W:aKUoWPGph-M 2024, QJnhnhXuuYM 2025]`. B2B, where reviews are rare: proposals, RFPs, sales-call notes, LinkedIn posts and comments `[G]`. Push for verbatim phrases `[MS]`.

**BRIEF-12 — Answer the four voice-of-customer questions** `[W:aKUoWPGph-M 2024]`:
1. **Trigger:** what happened that made them look for you?
2. **Perceived problem:** how do *they* name the problem? (It may differ from what you think you solve.)
3. **Alternatives:** what did they try or consider, including doing nothing?
4. **Objections:** what worried them before hiring you?
Add from `[W:rByridDOAKM 2025]`: **dream outcome** in their words. Add from `[MS]`: the JTBD forces (push away from the current situation, pull toward you, habit keeping them stuck, anxiety about switching).

**BRIEF-13 — Verify AI-mined language.** Wes uses ChatGPT/Perplexity/Deep Research to scan review sites for recurring phrases. The tools can invent or blend quotes (hallucination risk flagged in the notes, not by Wes). Keep only phrases you can trace to a real review or message; store the source URL. `[W:rByridDOAKM 2025, -n392pJo00I 2024] [G]`

**BRIEF-14 — Voice and locale.** Record formality (je/u, tu/vous), humor yes/no, words to use/avoid, target reading level (grade 7–8 (US) ≈ CEFR B1 / "klare taal" (NL) / "langage clair" (FR) default `[W:IQtpeWeVqt0 2026, dUrlwvm-CqQ 2024]`), languages and which is primary. Wes's default is casual and conversational for every industry: "speak to your prospects the way you'd speak to a friend" `[W:jPclFEiC2o4 2023]`. Formality is set per language and industry (`be-eu.md`).

**BRIEF-15 — Record traffic sources and awareness.** Cold search, paid social, Google Ads, referrals, YouTube, AI assistants, Google Business Profile. *Why:* paid traffic needs a dedicated page; warm traffic needs a shorter page (principle 15). Ask what share of leads say "I found you through ChatGPT / YouTube / a friend".

**BRIEF-16 — Record real constraints.** Capacity per month (for truthful scarcity), seasonality (for limited-edition offers), service area, regulated-profession advertising limits, languages the team can actually serve in. `[W:q_YbF26ji7w 2023] [G]`

**BRIEF-17 — Record assets.** Real team photos? Portfolio photos (must be sharp and high-res for visual trades)? Video? Logo as horizontal transparent PNG? Brand palette (to reserve an accent for the CTA)? *Why:* if the budget is limited, "always prioritize the photography" over the build `[W:RiNJ-rl4MSU 2022]`.

## Interview script (owner, 30–45 min)

Ask one block at a time; confirm each block back in one sentence `[MS]`.

1. **Business in one breath.** "If a friend asked at a barbecue what you do, what would you say?" (Listen for jargon. Write down the plain version.)
2. **Best client.** "Describe your favorite client of the last year. Why were they great? How did they find you? What were they struggling with when they called?" → BRIEF-02, trigger.
3. **Worst-fit client.** "Who do you wish would stop calling?" → anti-persona.
4. **The night before.** "What is your ideal client typing into Google or asking ChatGPT the night before they contact someone like you? What are they worried about?" → problem language, objections.
5. **After.** "Six months after working with you, what's different in their life? What do they tell their friends?" → after-state, image brief, benefits.
6. **Alternatives.** "What else do they try first? Why doesn't it work? What happens if they do nothing?" → comparison table, problem section.
7. **Why you.** "What do you do that others in your area don't, or don't talk about?" Then push: "What does that mean for the client?" (turn brags into benefits `[W:bYZkDOwQxfg 2022]`).
8. **Your process.** "Walk me through what happens from first contact to done." → compress to 3 steps later.
9. **Price.** "What does a typical job cost? What makes it cheaper or more expensive? What's the smallest and largest job you did this year?" → PRICE rules.
10. **Objections.** "What are the top three reasons people hesitate or say no?" → FAQ, wild card section, guarantee.
11. **Proof.** "How many clients so far? Reviews where? Can we quote three clients by name with a photo? Any numbers: time saved, money saved, results?"
12. **Capacity and season.** "How many new clients can you take per month? When are you slow?"
13. **Next step.** "When someone's ready, what exactly should they do? What happens after they do it, and how fast?"

## Heuristics for spotting a weak brief

- "Everyone" / "all businesses" / "homeowners" with no situation → push for the trigger event.
- "Quality, service, fair prices" as differentiators → these are table stakes; ask for a number, a guarantee, a process detail or a named method `[W:RlMBBbXzQ84 2023]`.
- "Tailored to your unique needs" → signals "I don't have a real process" `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`; ask for the process.
- The owner's favorite line is a slogan, pun or mission statement → keep it for the About page `[W:64ie_-xuMgQ 2021]`.
- No price information at all → record the reason; propose ranges or factors.
- Proof = "people love us" → list concrete sources or schedule collection.
