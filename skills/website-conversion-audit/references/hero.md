# 20 — Hero / above the fold

The hero is Wes's most consistent teaching from 2018 to 2025: tell people **what you do, why it matters to them, and what to do next** in about five seconds `[W:b0KQaZkDBKU 2018, nfFlB0pmsWc 2020, 8ZX9y8GP0xs 2022, jsOoglMSpdY 2024, Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]`. From 2023 he adds a fourth job, **why you** ("probably even more important" than clarity) `[W:2lYdI86YV30 2023]`. He claims getting the hero right is "80% of the way" and that structure alone made one homepage convert 3× better (both illustrative) `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024, UMDUqWxstow 2025]`.

## Anatomy (top to bottom)

| Slot | Job | Required? |
|---|---|---|
| Logo (top left, normal size) + nav (≤5) + nav CTA button (top right) | Identification + an always-available next step | Yes (no nav on paid LPs) |
| Eyebrow | "You're in the right place": audience or situation | Recommended |
| Headline | Result, problem, or what-you-do (by formula) | Yes |
| Subheadline | The missing half: what/how + who + format/timeline + location | Yes, "~99% of the time" `[W:64ie_-xuMgQ 2021]` |
| Outcome row | 3 checkmarked outcomes | Recommended `[W:Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]` |
| Primary CTA + booster line | Specific next step + risk remover | Yes |
| Hero proof | Stars + count, client faces, one stat or mini quote | Recommended `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]` |
| Image | Ideal customer in the after-state | Yes |

## HERO rules

**HERO-01 — Pass the 5-second test.** Within ~5 seconds a stranger can say (1) what you do, (2) why it matters to them, (3) what to do next, and ideally (4) why you over others. *Why:* visitors scan for signs they're in the right place ("a billboard in the desert on the way to Vegas"); confused people don't scroll to find clarity, they leave `[W:w79jVvGlIDE 2023, 8ZX9y8GP0xs 2022]`. The 2020 version adds a fourth check: **is the image helping to tell that story, or confusing it?** `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020]`. Protocol below.

**HERO-02 — Pick the headline formula by business type** `[W:64ie_-xuMgQ 2021]`:

| Formula | Use when | Template | Example (Wes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Show the transformation (strongest) | You change a life, body, home or business: fitness, coaching, counseling, interiors, landscaping | "We'll take you from ___ to ___" / "[After-state]" | "Get into the best shape of your life" + "We're a CrossFit studio specializing in group workouts that get you lasting results" |
| 2. State the problem you solve | You fix a real problem without "transforming": locksmith, attorney, pest control, immigration | "[Problem] can be [pain]. We make it [easy/safe]." / "Stop [pain]" | "Immigration issues can be complicated. We make it easy." + "Whether you need a green card or a visa, our skilled attorneys will guide you through the entire process safely to the other side." |
| 3. Say what you do and why it matters (fallback) | Novel concept, or utilitarian need-based service where visitors arrive ready to buy | "[Plain what] that [why it matters]" | "Live chat that connects your website visitors with your happiest customers"; "Modern dentistry with a smile" `[W:8ZX9y8GP0xs 2022]` |

Prefer 1 or 2 whenever possible; they are "always going to be more powerful". Either order works: benefit first with the what in the subhead ("Never wax your car again" + "Paint protection services for a long-lasting, maintenance-free finish") is fine as long as the other half follows immediately `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020]`.

**HERO-03 — Anchor on the customer's biggest desire, not your category.** "Nobody's desire is a gym… their desire is to get in shape." "Nobody wants extermination; they want to feel safe and clean in their own home" → "We'll protect your home and your family from unwanted pests" `[W:64ie_-xuMgQ 2021]`. Lifestyle over utility: a garden-maintenance list became "Effortlessly beautiful gardens all year round" `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`.

**HERO-04 — Use their words for the pain.** Verbatim customer phrases beat invented slogans: rehab center "Your addiction ends here" → "If you think you need rehab, you do" (Wes relays "400%+ more inquiries", illustrative); "Streamline your business operations" → "Stop drowning in admin work" `[W:aKUoWPGph-M 2024, rByridDOAKM 2025]`.

**HERO-05 — Avoid the seven headline failures** `[W:64ie_-xuMgQ 2021]`:

| Failure | Tell | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Mysterious | "Your financial education starts right now" | Say the result and the offer |
| Too clever | "It's a beautiful day in the neighborhood" (brand designer) | Move wordplay to About; say what you do |
| Copycat cliché | "Boosting brands through digital design" | Name the problem, then "we can help, here's how" |
| Jargon | "…using our peer-to-peer chat" | Replace the term with what it does |
| Leaving them hanging | "Are you ready for adulting?" alone | Keep it, add a concrete subhead (a joke needs a punch line) |
| Vague platitude | "Your life changes here" (law firm) | Problem → claim → how |
| Obvious SEO play | "Professional pest control exterminator servicing Morris, Warren and Sussex County" | Human promise in the headline; keywords + towns in the subhead |

Also banned in the hero: "Welcome to [business]" `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021]`, awards and "voted #1" (nobody cares about you yet) `[W:8ZX9y8GP0xs 2022]`, filler like "Kansas City's preferred DJ service" `[W:7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`, rotating taglines without a concrete follow-up `[W:2lYdI86YV30 2023]`, an unexplained proprietary framework name `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`, insider slogans visitors can't decode `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020]`.

**HERO-06 — Subheadline supplies what/how + who, plus format and place when relevant.** Headline = result; subhead = what you actually offer and how, and who you specialize in `[W:w79jVvGlIDE 2023, 2lYdI86YV30 2023]`. Add the **format/timeline** for packaged services so buyers know what they're signing up for: "I provide counseling to Texas professionals in four sessions…" `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020]`, "4 weeks to lasting change" `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. Keep the **location** for local SEO ("Texas", "Parker County") `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. Answer the "why?" your differentiator raises (a trainer who uses no treats says why treats are bad) `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020]`. Length: one sentence. A crowded hero (headline + subhead + several sentences) gets cut to headline + one supporting sentence + button `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020]`.

**HERO-07 — Keywords go in the subhead, not the headline.** Wes's 2021 trick: style the promise as the big visual headline but tag it `<h2>`, tag the keyword + city subhead `<h1>` `[W:64ie_-xuMgQ 2021]`. *Caveat [G]:* this puts headings out of order (an accessibility smell). Prefer an `<h1>` that holds the promise with the service + city in a smaller span inside it, or rely on the title tag and a subhead paragraph. Search engines match on the whole page, not only the H1.

**HERO-08 — Add an eyebrow line.** A short line above the headline naming the audience or situation ("For Ghent homeowners planning a renovation") confirms "you're in the right place" before the headline is read and helps skimmers. Especially useful on paid LPs, where it can echo the ad `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`.

**HERO-09 — Add three outcome checkmarks.** A row of three short outcomes the client can expect: not features, "not really even benefits", *outcomes* ("More clients / Clear authority / Bigger opportunities"; "Ditch the 9-to-5 / Grow your fan base / Turn passion into paychecks") `[W:Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025, UMDUqWxstow 2025, g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`.

**HERO-10 — Exactly one primary button, specific wording, plus a booster line.** One filled button ("I think having the one is best"; delete the builder's second "Learn" button) `[W:trfDoGoXNsE 2025]`. Wording = action verb + what happens next ("Schedule your free discovery call", "Get your free estimate", "Book your free trial walk") `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020, g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025, trfDoGoXNsE 2025]`. Under it, a one-line reassurance ("Free, 20 minutes, no obligation"), which Wes calls his #1 quick win `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`. Full rules in `cta-forms.md`. *Except:* the transitional offer may sit **below** as a text link for not-ready visitors (C1 in `principles.md`); never as the main button `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`.

**HERO-11 — Put proof in the hero.** Options: row of 4–6 real client photos + five stars + "Over 500 happy clients and counting"; Google rating + review count; one mini testimonial as a pull quote; a stat callout (people helped, five-star reviews, money saved); client or earned media logos for B2B `[W:Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025, UMDUqWxstow 2025, mGbLhEY7nPE 2024, tIHQFcmYptw 2024]`. A strong result quote can even be the headline ("This service saved me hours every week and doubled my leads") `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`. Use real, current numbers; Wes openly made up his demo numbers, and real sites must not `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`.

**HERO-12 — Image = the ideal customer in the after-state.** Show the happy ending, not the service being performed ("don't show the sausage being made"): family enjoying the yard, not the landscaper mowing; patient cycling, not the physio room; homeowner outside under a quality roof; client on their porch with coffee, not the planner in a tie `[W:K5is_yACb7M 2024, 7kG8otQJfws 2021, g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025, Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]`. The image must match the offer: a family in a living room confuses on a roofer's site `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. "The words sell more than the image; the image is window dressing", so it supports the headline and never competes with it `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024, UMDUqWxstow 2025]`.

**HERO-13 — Image craft.** `[W:OvQvDu5ZKzQ 2020, w79jVvGlIDE 2023, ClD037qJSTQ 2025, dUrlwvm-CqQ 2024]`
- Match the people to the target demographic (age, family situation, gender mix) so visitors recognize themselves.
- Natural "captured moment" expressions: satisfied, relieved, peaceful. No "caricature of happy" (fist-pumping, jumping, screaming).
- Prefer a face, forward-facing (reads as inviting next to the CTA); if a person looks sideways, make their eyeline point at the headline or CTA `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024]`.
- Big and sharp: at least one column wide; ~1,900 px wide for full-width heroes. Blurry images cost credibility.
- Vibrant, not muddy; pick a series from the same shoot or the same photographer's portfolio for consistency `[W:8ZX9y8GP0xs 2022]`.
- Don't oversell (no "making it rain" for a financial planner).
- Stock search method: after-state scene + emotion words ("bathroom smiling", "dog owner biking"), then filter out posed shots. Test with watermarked previews in the real layout first.
- Never in the hero: you or your team, skylines, building exteriors, empty offices, equipment, abstract tech, word clouds, text-in-image stock.
- AI-generated customer/mood images are acceptable if natural; check for artifacts (three leashes) `[W:trfDoGoXNsE 2025, pRNny_t8TUE 2025]`.

**HERO-14 — You in the hero only as the exception.** Personal brand where you *are* the product: test a shot of you leading clients, with clients in frame `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020]`. A known personality (TV credential) may show themselves in action, "one of the only times" `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. Everyone else: a therapist or coach never puts their own portrait in the hero `[W:2lYdI86YV30 2023]`.

**HERO-15 — Visual businesses show the work, varied.** Designers, contractors and landscapers: show the *differentiating* work (decorative stamped floors, not a plain garage) `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020]`. Use a multi-image grid so one piece not in the visitor's style doesn't cause a back-button `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024]`. Portfolio photos must be real and sharp `[W:ClD037qJSTQ 2025]`.

**HERO-16 — Layout: split, text left, image right.** Western readers scan left to right; people spend "~80% more time" on the left (older desktop eye-tracking; illustrative, weaker on mobile). Headline, subhead and CTA go left. "Don't get cute with right-hand menus or left-hand hero images." `[W:w79jVvGlIDE 2023, UMDUqWxstow 2025]`. Text over a background image is "generally not recommended anymore": hard to read, text crosses faces as the browser resizes `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024]`. *Except:* faceless images or video (spaces, groups, landscapes) with a dark overlay or a solid content box `[W:UMDUqWxstow 2025]`.

**HERO-17 — Logo top left at normal size, never in the hero body.** A giant or doubled logo "doesn't help anybody make a decision" `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020, 2lYdI86YV30 2023]`. Horizontal format, transparent PNG/SVG `[W:trfDoGoXNsE 2025]`.

**HERO-18 — No sliders or carousels; one message.** `[W:_hBEZT8-uOw 2018]`. (Wes used slideshows in 2020 as a workaround for multiple avatars; he later replaced them with static multi-image grids `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024]`.)

**HERO-19 — Background video: calm or nothing.** One ambient, purposeful clip that matches the headline's emotional promise (a generic dog video doesn't sell "coming home to a calm, happy dog"), dark overlay, no fast cuts; you need rights to the footage `[W:2lYdI86YV30 2023, trfDoGoXNsE 2025, UMDUqWxstow 2025]`. On mobile, swap to a static image if it misbehaves `[W:7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`. Weigh against load time (`page-structure.md`).

**HERO-20 — Remove competing hero elements.** Review widgets, chat bubbles, pop-ups and extra text fighting for attention dilute the message `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021]`. Chat/welcome pop-ups firing immediately make people "scramble for the back button"; delay them 1–2 minutes or use exit intent `[W:7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`. *[G, BE/EU]:* the cookie banner is the biggest fold-eater on mobile; see `be-eu.md`.

**HERO-21 — Mobile hero: one image, stacked, CTA visible.** Pick the single strongest image for mobile; stack text above image; bento tiles can be reordered or dropped on mobile `[W:UMDUqWxstow 2025, tIHQFcmYptw 2024]`. [G]: on a 390×844 viewport, headline + subhead + button should be visible without scrolling, with the consent banner shown.

## Hero layouts by business type

From the nine 2024 layouts and the 2025 update `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024, UMDUqWxstow 2025]`. "Familiar enough to be intuitive, unique enough to stand out."

| Layout | Best for | Contents | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Split** (default) | Most service businesses | Text left, happy-customer image right | Text must stay left |
| **Text above image + stat + results** | Clean modern services, coaches, B2B services | Headline/sub/CTA stacked over a wide image, stat callout ("150+ coaches like you"), 3 result bullets | Image tells the after-story (client holding their book) |
| **Social-proof hero** | Consulting, clinics, any business with many reviews | Stars + client faces or logos inside the hero | Only real reviews |
| **Multi-image / multi-avatar** | Several customer types or age ranges (tutoring, speech therapy) | 2–3 customer photos + stat + results | One image on mobile |
| **Portfolio grid** | Designers, contractors, landscapers | Varied work samples | Real photos only |
| **Full-width faceless image/video + overlay box** | Spaces, outdoor builds, venues | Overlay box with 3 results + avatar strip + stars | Dark overlay, no close-up faces |
| **Hero with inline form** | Urgent/utilitarian trades (locksmith, plumber) and some realtors | Headline, subhead, image + 2–3 field form, repeated at page bottom | "I don't always recommend a treatment like this." [G]: add click-to-call |
| **Services/results bullets + primary and ghost button** | Accountants, multi-service firms | Three services or results under the headline | Ghost button stays visually subordinate (C1) |
| **Bento** | Color-confident brands with a designer | Modular tiles: image, stat, mini testimonial, review count | Few tiles, or "the eye doesn't know where to look"; stacks well on mobile |
| **Show-not-tell result** | B2B services, agencies | Device mockup or screenshot of a real result instead of a plain stat | Real data |

Illustration instead of photography can add personality (a playful exterminator) `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024]`.

## 5-second test protocol

This section is the single source for how the 5-second test is run, scored and graded; other files point here. [G] procedure built on Wes's criteria `[W:b0KQaZkDBKU 2018, nfFlB0pmsWc 2020, 2lYdI86YV30 2023]`:

1. Screenshot the hero at desktop 1440 px **and** mobile 390 px, cookie banner visible.
2. Show it for 5 seconds to 3–5 people who don't know the business (or, as a skill, simulate: read only what is visible, in visual order, **before** reading any business context or strategy docs).
3. Ask: What does this business do? For whom? Why would you pick them? What would you click, and what happens then?
4. **Real-people pass:** ≥ 4 of 5 answer "what" and "next step" correctly, and ≥ 3 can name a benefit or reason. Then score the rubric below to set severity.
5. Also note: is the image helping or confusing? Is there any proof? Is the CTA the most prominent element after the headline? Does a different filled button (e.g. in the nav) compete with the hero CTA?

**Rubric** (0–2 per criterion, scored separately for desktop and mobile; the lower total counts):

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| What | Unclear | Category only | Specific service + who |
| Why it matters | Absent | Generic benefit | Concrete outcome in customer language |
| Next step | No CTA / vague | Specific but weak visually, or a competing filled button nearby | Specific, standout, booster |
| Why you | Absent | Generic claim | Differentiator or proof visible |
| Image | Confusing or about you; **only a logo, abstract art, pattern or decoration** (score 0 and say so) | Neutral: relevant but generic | After-state, on-avatar. B2B/software: the real product or deliverable in use by the target user |

**Pass and severity (A-M1):** PASS = ≥ 7/10 with no 0 on What or Next step. **≤ 4/10, or a 0 on What or Next step → Critical**: rewrite the hero first. **5–6/10 → High**: fix the weak criteria in the High-impact list (or as quick wins when only Why you or Image is low).
