# 24 — CTAs, booking vs forms, form fields, thank-you page

Frame: the page gives enough information that the visitor's only remaining question is "how do I get this?", and the CTA answers it `[W:n5Sk2sz8E2Y 2022]`. Wes calls the CTA rules "the number one piece of advice I give that gets ignored almost every time" `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021, kZdXTq5uOEI 2021]`. Many SMB homepages have no CTA at all (Wes: 70%; illustrative) `[W:n5Sk2sz8E2Y 2022]`.

## CTA rules

**CTA-01 — One primary action per site (and per page).** Decide it in the brief: call, book a consult, get a quote. One offer, one CTA, one clear path `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018, b0KQaZkDBKU 2018]`. The transitional offer is a secondary path, never the primary (`CTA-10`).

**CTA-02 — Wording = action verb + what happens next (+ "free" when true).** Use book / get / schedule / reserve + the concrete thing: "Book a free consultation", "Schedule your free discovery call", "Get your free estimate", "Book your free trial walk", "Get my free checklist", "Come in for a free class" `[W:n5Sk2sz8E2Y 2022, nfFlB0pmsWc 2020, kZdXTq5uOEI 2021, trfDoGoXNsE 2025]`. *Why:* vague buttons leave people unsure what follows (a form? a chat? a call at dinner time? what's the conversation about?), which "subconsciously stops them" `[W:n5Sk2sz8E2Y 2022]`. Name the format of free offers ("Free training" → "Get the free video training") `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020]`. `[MS]` formula: [Action verb] + [what they get] + [qualifier].

**CTA-03 — Banned CTA copy.** Learn more, Find out more, Find out here (passive) · Get started (what does it involve?) · Contact us (for what?) · Submit · Click here · Get connected · clever lines ("Birth boldly", "Unleash better health") · **misleading** lines ("Chat with a vet now" that opens a form) `[W:nfFlB0pmsWc 2020, t4gF4aVRcDY 2024, Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025, g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. Specificity is a spectrum: "Book now" is OK, "Book an appointment" / "Book a free consultation" is best `[W:8ZX9y8GP0xs 2022]`.

**CTA-04 — Identical everywhere.** Same text, color and style every time, on every page; copy-paste the hero button into the closing section `[W:n5Sk2sz8E2Y 2022, kZdXTq5uOEI 2021, trfDoGoXNsE 2025]`. Four differently worded and styled buttons on one page confuse `[W:8ZX9y8GP0xs 2022]`. "Think of it like the traffic light of your website": blue means book a call.

**CTA-05 — A button color used nowhere else.** Bold, fits the palette, clearly stands out; not in the logo, icons, headings or accents; not the brand/logo color ("if the logo is blue, the button isn't blue") `[W:n5Sk2sz8E2Y 2022, kZdXTq5uOEI 2021, 8ZX9y8GP0xs 2022, _hBEZT8-uOw 2018]`. Filled, not transparent/ghost, for the primary `[W:trfDoGoXNsE 2025]`. Not black or low-contrast ("fades into the background") `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021, 2lYdI86YV30 2023]`. A subtle glow is OK on busy backgrounds; animated or arrow-laden buttons feel "scammy" `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. [G]: text/background contrast ≥ 4.5:1.

**CTA-06 — Repeat it down the page.** Positions: nav (top right, styled as the button), hero, after benefits/process, after testimonials, the final CTA section, footer; optionally a sticky header `[W:K5is_yACb7M 2024, _hBEZT8-uOw 2018, 7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`. People hit "ready" at different depths. Several CTAs on long pages (Wes: "up to 220% more leads"; illustrative) `[W:3ahiFlIHvDc 2022]`.

**CTA-07 — Give it room.** White space around the button (~80 px margin) `[W:8ZX9y8GP0xs 2022]`; negative space around CTAs (Wes: "232%"; illustrative) `[W:3ahiFlIHvDc 2022]`. The CTA must not compete with "15 other visually interesting things" `[W:pRNny_t8TUE 2025]`.

**CTA-08 — Booster line under the button.** A small reassurance line that removes "what if I make the wrong choice?": Wes's #1 quick win `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`. Service-business versions: "Free · 20 minutes · no obligation" · "Pick a time that suits you, online" · "We reply within 1 working day" · "No sales pitch: just a plan you can use" · "Quote is free and fixed". (SaaS examples like "No credit card required" rarely fit services.)

**CTA-09 — The process section's Step 1 uses the CTA words.** "Step 1: Schedule a consultation" + what they do + its benefit `[W:Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]`. The final section asks a question ("Are you ready to…?") above the same button `[W:Zxz-0r8G7KQ 2025]`; as a TL;DR panel it restates only the offer, the main benefit and one reason `[W:mGbLhEY7nPE 2024]`.

**CTA-10 — Secondary / transitional CTA rules.** Not everyone is ready to book: give them a smaller next step `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024, bWVyN9VoBB4 2026]`. It must be visually subordinate (ghost button or text link) `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024]` and must never hide the main path: a quiz as the hero's main CTA "adds mystery and hides the conversion path" `[W:g_Wk6nJD1_Y 2025]`. Placement: under the hero CTA as a text link (homepage only; C1), in its own lower section, on content pages, in exit intent (desktop). Vary ask sizes across the site and content (small: free download; medium: quiz/estimate; big: book) so there's "always a next step available, whatever size they're ready for" `[W:IQtpeWeVqt0 2026]`.

**CTA-11 — First person is a test, not a rule.** "Start my free trial" beat "Start your free trial" in Michael Aagaard's test (+90%; illustrative) `[W:n5Sk2sz8E2Y 2022, 3ahiFlIHvDc 2022]`.

**CTA-12 — The click must deliver what the button said.** If it says "Book", the next screen is a calendar, not a form and a promised callback `[W:t4gF4aVRcDY 2024]`.

## After the click: booking vs form vs call

Wes's arc: "fill in a form and we call you" → scheduler, because scheduled conversations beat callbacks that hit prospects while cooking dinner; it "turns 'what now' into 'see you then'" `[W:7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019, K5is_yACb7M 2024, dUrlwvm-CqQ 2024]`.

| Business model | Primary path | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consult / discovery call / appointment (coach, therapist, advisor, clinic, agency) | **Self-booking scheduler** embedded on the page or a dedicated booking page, synced with your calendar, with email/SMS reminders | Visitors see availability and control timing; no back-and-forth; fewer no-shows; no double bookings | `[W:ClD037qJSTQ 2025, dUrlwvm-CqQ 2024, 7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]` |
| Quote-based job (renovation, landscaping, removals) | **Short quote form** (or an estimator that ends in a request) | You need a few facts to quote; keep it to the bare minimum | `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021, _2gU7G5U99I 2025]` |
| Urgent / emergency (locksmith, plumber, towing) | **Click-to-call** + 2–3 field callback form in the hero | Speed matters more than information | `[W:tIHQFcmYptw 2024]` [G] |
| Premium, overbooked, application-based | **Short application** ("a little tiny bit of friction") | Filters for best clients; a huge questionnaire kills completion | `[W:q_YbF26ji7w 2023, 7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]` |
| Any | **Never** a vague "Contact us" form with no expectation of what happens next | "Mystery is a turnoff" | `[W:dUrlwvm-CqQ 2024]` |
| Any | **Never** a `mailto:` link as the only path. The label promises a conversation ("Let's talk", "Plan een gesprek"), but the click opens an email draft, or nothing when no mail client is set up, and conversions go untracked | Breaks CTA-12 and leaves you blind (A-T4). Instead: a scheduler or a short form per the rows above; if email stays, label it as email ("Mail us at info@…") and track the click. All CTAs mailto → A-F1, High | [G] |

Also: don't list a bare email address as the contact path (scrapers and spam); a form lets you ask a qualifying question and gives you context before replying `[W:ClD037qJSTQ 2025, trfDoGoXNsE 2025]`. [G/BE]: EU law still requires an email address in the legal info; put it in the legal notice/footer (`be-eu.md`).

**BOOK-01 — Remove extra clicks.** After "Check availability" says yes, show the next step immediately and prefill what they already entered (the date); don't make them click "Book now" to reach another form `[W:7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`.

**BOOK-02 — Set expectations at every step.** Duration, format (phone, video, on site), who they'll talk to, what to prepare, what it costs (free?), when you'll respond `[W:K5is_yACb7M 2024]` `[MS]`.

**BOOK-03 — Personal welcome after booking.** A 20–30-second personalized video greeting by name, what to expect on the call, what to prepare: raises show-up rates ("dramatically"; illustrative) `[W:qvbLxQsic4w 2022]`. AI scheduling assistants that propose times by email/chat, follow your rules and send reminders are the 2025 upgrade `[W:_2gU7G5U99I 2025]`.

**BOOK-04 — Chat.** Visitors who won't call or email will ask in chat, even on a first visit `[W:qvbLxQsic4w 2022]`. But people expect replies in 30–60 seconds; slow human chat is a bad first impression, so use an AI assistant trained on your services, prices, FAQs and past chats, with your tone configured `[W:_2gU7G5U99I 2025, 1kgolxbvCd8 ≤2024]`. Never let a chat bubble pop open on arrival (`HERO-20`).

## Form field rules

Wes's rules are few and blunt; `[MS]` fills in form UX.

**FORM-01 — Ask the minimum for the first contact.** First name, email (or phone if phone is how you follow up), plus maybe one soft qualifying or emotional question ("What is your dream wedding like?") `[W:7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`. "The less you ask for, the better." Quote forms: only what you need to give a basic quote; too many options (vehicle sizes, service types) and people shut down and defer `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021]`. Wes's "three fields is the magic number, each extra field halves conversion" is not reliable (illustrative) `[W:t4gF4aVRcDY 2024]`; `[MS]` rule of thumb: 3 fields baseline, 4–6 fields ≈ 10–25% fewer completions, 7+ ≈ 25–50%+ fewer (directional).

**FORM-02 — Justify every field.** Is it needed *before* you can help? Can you get it later (on the call, in onboarding) or another way? `[MS]`. Drop the phone field if you'll reply by email (people hesitate to give phone numbers); drop "business or personal" if unneeded `[W:7kG8otQJfws 2021]`. Long intake forms come **after** first contact `[W:7hhQf3T3k-Q 2019]`.

**FORM-03 — Order and layout** `[MS]`: easy fields first (name, email), sensitive last (phone, budget); single column; visible labels above fields (placeholders are examples, not labels); conversational labels ("What's the project?"); radio buttons for < 5 options; "Other" with a text field; optional fields marked.

**FORM-04 — Multi-step for longer forms** `[MS]`: when more than ~5–6 fields or conditional paths (quote requests): progress indicator, one topic per step, easy first and sensitive last, back navigation, keep entered data on refresh. Start with a low-friction question (service type as big buttons) to build commitment.

**FORM-05 — Validation and errors** `[MS]`: validate when the user leaves a field, not while typing; specific error text next to the field with how to fix it ("Enter an email like name@example.be"); never clear their input; on submit, focus the first error.

**FORM-06 — Mobile** `[MS]`: tap targets ≥ 44 px; correct keyboard types (`type="email"`, `type="tel"`); autofill attributes (`autocomplete="name|email|tel|postal-code"`); submit button visible without hunting.

**FORM-07 — Button and surroundings.** Button text = action + what they get ("Get my free quote", "Send my request"), never "Submit" `[MS]` `[W:n5Sk2sz8E2Y 2022]`. Near the form: expected response time, a short privacy line, a testimonial or rating, and alternatives (phone) `[MS]`. [G/BE]: privacy-notice link, separate unticked marketing consent (`be-eu.md`).

**FORM-08 — Post-submit states** `[MS]`: disable the button with a loading state; on success go to a thank-you page (not just a small inline message) so you can set expectations and track the conversion; on error, a clear message and preserved data.

## Thank-you page

The step most sites waste `[MS]`. Wes's foot-in-the-door play: after the small yes, present the bigger next step `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018]`.

**THANKS-01 — Confirm and set expectations.** "Got it, [name]." What happens next, by when, from whom, through which channel ("Check your inbox; the guide is there within 2 minutes"; "Sarah calls you tomorrow before noon from our office number") `[MS]` `[W:K5is_yACb7M 2024]`.

**THANKS-02 — Offer the next logical step, one size up.** Lead-magnet opt-in → offer a free call or the estimate; booking → prep checklist + a short welcome video + "while you wait" proof (a case study that matches their situation) `[W:dKn4Zxpu9jk 2018, qvbLxQsic4w 2022]` `[MS]`.

**THANKS-03 — Keep them in your world, not on social.** Link to your best content (logistics answers, case studies), not to your Instagram `[W:dUrlwvm-CqQ 2024]`.

**THANKS-04 — Make it measurable [G].** Unique URL (or event) per conversion type (booking, quote, call click, opt-in) so analytics and ad platforms count real conversions; exclude it from search indexing.

## CTA and form audit checks (summary)

- One primary action; wording specific; no banned phrases; identical everywhere; in nav top right; in hero; ≥ 3 repeats on a long page; reserved color, high contrast, filled; booster line; secondary visually subordinate; click delivers what the label promised.
- Booking path matches the business model; no mystery contact form; ≤ 3–4 first-contact fields (+1 qualifier); labels visible; mobile keyboards; errors helpful; thank-you page with next step; conversion tracked.
